Power & heart-rate zones, side by side

Power reacts instantly and is the primary reference. HR lags behind effort by 30–90 seconds, so it's most useful for steady zones (1–5) and unreliable for anything shorter than about 3 minutes.

How exact is this alignment? Power zones are exact — they're percentages of your tested FTP. HR zones here are estimated from resting/max HR (the Karvonen method), not a measured lactate-threshold HR, so treat them as approximate. Alignment is fairly reliable for Z1–Z3 (steady efforts, long enough for HR to settle) and reasonable for Z4 (anchored near the same ~1-hour effort FTP represents), but gets shakier for Z5 and isn't meaningful for Z6–Z7 — those efforts are over before HR catches up. When in doubt, ride to power, not the HR number.

Putting it into a week

Two sample structures at different weekly volumes. Both follow a polarized shape rather than spreading effort evenly.

The 80/20 rule: in most well-tested endurance programs, roughly 80% of weekly time sits in Zone 1–2 (easy), and the remaining 20% is genuinely hard (Zone 4 and up). Zone 3 "tempo" is used sparingly — it's tiring enough to blunt recovery but not intense enough to drive the same adaptations as real threshold or VO2max work, so it's easy to overuse and under-benefit from.

A sequence built for visceral fat loss

Why this mix, not just "burn more calories": visceral fat carries more beta-adrenergic receptors than the fat under your skin, so it responds disproportionately to the adrenaline spike from harder efforts — several trials show interval work outperforming steady cardio for visceral/abdominal fat specifically, at matched total calories. But daily high intensity also raises cortisol, which works against you. So this plan leans on volume (Session C) as the base, with intensity (Sessions A & B) layered on top rather than replacing it. Diet and a calorie deficit are still doing most of the work here — this is the exercise side of the equation.

A sequence built for subcutaneous fat loss

Watt targets scale automatically with your FTP input at the top of the page.

Why this looks different from the visceral plan: subcutaneous fat — especially in the classically "stubborn" spots — carries more alpha-2 receptors, which actively resist the same adrenaline signal that mobilizes visceral fat so readily. It responds less to spikes of intensity and more to sheer volume and duration at a fat-oxidizing pace, sustained over weeks. So the emphasis flips here: more Zone 2 time, more frequency, less reliance on hard intervals to do the work. Two things worth knowing: there's no such thing as spot reduction — where fat comes off first is mostly genetics, not which muscles you work — and the "fasted cardio burns more fat" idea has mixed evidence at best once you look at fat loss over a full day rather than just during the ride. Also worth knowing, even though it's outside a cardio plan: resistance training is one of the most reliable levers for subcutaneous fat specifically, via muscle retention and resting metabolic rate — worth pairing with this if it isn't already part of your week.

Holding it, once you're there

Watt targets scale automatically with your FTP input at the top of the page.

The lever here is intensity, not volume. Classic detraining research (Hickson et al.) found that cutting training frequency or duration by two-thirds — while keeping intensity the same — held VO2max and FTP-type fitness for months. Cutting intensity instead, even at the same volume, is what actually lets fitness slide. So maintenance isn't a smaller version of the build-up plans above — it's the same hard effort, done far less often, with everything else stripped away. One consistent interval session a week is a genuine floor; two gives more margin.

Body fat % from here on is mostly a food question, not a training one: at maintenance calories, no exercise volume substitutes for that. What training still buys you at this stage is fitness (so FTP doesn't quietly drift down) and some help with appetite regulation and daily energy balance, which supports not creeping back into a surplus. One honest note: metabolism sometimes runs a bit more efficient after a period of fat loss, so don't be surprised if holding steady takes a little more daily movement than it used to. If FTP starts sliding over a few months, that's the signal to add the second session back — not a sign anything's gone wrong.

Method: power zones use the standard 7-zone model (percentages of FTP). Heart-rate zones use the Karvonen / heart-rate-reserve method — target HR = resting HR + (% × [max HR − resting HR]) — which is more accurate than straight %-of-max because it accounts for your individual resting HR. VO₂max is estimated from the Uth–Sørensen–Overgaard–Pedersen formula (15.3 × max HR ÷ resting HR), a rough non-exercise estimate, not a substitute for lab testing.

These are general training guidelines, not individual coaching. Response to intensity varies by person — if you're new to structured high-intensity work, introduce it gradually, and check with a doctor before starting if you have any cardiovascular risk factors.
