Zones›

Power reacts instantly; HR lags 30–90s, so it's most useful for zones 1–5.

How exact is this? Power zones are exact — % of your tested FTP. HR zones are estimated (Karvonen method), not a measured lactate threshold. Reliable for Z1–Z3, reasonable for Z4, shakier for Z5, and not meaningful for Z6–Z7 — those efforts end before HR catches up. When in doubt, ride to power.

Weekly Structure›
The 80/20 rule: ~80% of weekly time in Zone 1–2, the rest genuinely hard (Zone 4+). Zone 3 "tempo" is used sparingly — tiring enough to blunt recovery, not intense enough to drive the same adaptations.

Visceral Fat Loss›
Why this mix: visceral fat has more beta-adrenergic receptors than fat under the skin, so it responds disproportionately to the adrenaline spike from hard efforts — intervals outperform steady cardio here at matched calories. But daily high intensity raises cortisol, which works against you, so volume (Session C) stays the base with intensity (A & B) layered on top. Diet still does most of the work.

Sample week

5–6 sessions, ~5–6.5 hr. Start with 1× Session A/week if new to this.

Subcutaneous Fat Loss›
Why this differs: subcutaneous fat — especially "stubborn" spots — carries more alpha-2 receptors, resisting the same adrenaline signal. It responds more to volume and duration than intensity spikes. No spot reduction exists, and "fasted cardio burns more fat" has mixed evidence once you look at a full day. Resistance training also helps here, via muscle retention — worth pairing in even though it's outside a cardio plan.

Sample week

6–7 sessions, ~7–8 hr. Heavier on volume, lighter on hard intervals.

Cardio Maintenance›
The lever is intensity, not volume. Detraining research (Hickson et al.) found cutting frequency/duration by two-thirds while keeping intensity held VO2max/FTP for months — cutting intensity is what lets fitness slide. So this isn't a smaller build-up plan; it's the same hard effort, done far less often. Body fat % from here is mostly a food question — maintenance calories, not more cardio. If FTP drifts down over months, add the second session back.

Method: power zones use the standard 7-zone model (% of FTP). HR zones use the Karvonen / heart-rate-reserve method — target HR = resting HR + (% × [max HR − resting HR]). VO₂max is estimated from the Uth–Sørensen–Overgaard–Pedersen formula (15.3 × max HR ÷ resting HR), a rough field estimate, not a lab value.

General guidelines, not individual coaching. Introduce high intensity gradually if you're new to it, and check with a doctor first if you have cardiovascular risk factors.
